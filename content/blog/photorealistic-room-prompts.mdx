Photorealism is not just a rendering style. It is a discipline of specificity. The more clearly you describe light, materials, and camera perspective, the more believable the result becomes.

The goal is not to force a tool into taste. The goal is to give it enough design intelligence to avoid generic output.

## Name the Camera

A convincing room image usually needs a realistic point of view. Ask for an eye-level interior photograph, natural lens compression, soft daylight, and true material texture.

Avoid impossible aerial angles unless the purpose is floor planning.

## Describe Material Hierarchy

A polished prompt separates dominant, secondary, and accent materials. For example: limewashed walls as the dominant surface, walnut as the secondary material, and blackened brass as the accent.

That hierarchy keeps the room from becoming a catalogue of unrelated finishes.

## Say What to Exclude

Negative direction is part of taste. Ask the system to avoid clutter, exaggerated reflections, overfilled shelves, distorted furniture, and harsh artificial lighting.

<Callout>
The most elegant AI room prompt is often less about style labels and more about restraint.
</Callout>

Once the first image appears, edit the prompt in small moves. Change one variable at a time so you know what improved the room.
